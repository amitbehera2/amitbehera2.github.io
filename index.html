Copy-Protection with Correlated Challenges: Point Functions and More via Decisional Coset Monogamy Amit Behera, Alper Çakan, and Vipul Goyal arXiv 2026
Abstract and links

This work introduces correlated-challenge security for single-decryptor encryption and unclonable puncturable obfuscation, allowing the two challenge instances to be arbitrarily correlated. It proves correlated security for existing constructions under standard cryptographic assumptions and obtains the first plain-model copy-protection schemes for point functions, k-point functions, and compute-and-compare programs, as well as identical-challenge copy-protection for general puncturable functionalities.

Copy-Protection from Unclonable Puncturable Obfuscation, Revisited Prabhanjan Ananth, Amit Behera, Zikuan Huang, Fuyuki Kitagawa, and Takashi Yamakawa EUROCRYPT 2026
Abstract and links

Quantum copy-protection compiles a classical program into a functionality-preserving quantum program that cannot be duplicated. Assuming indistinguishability obfuscation and Learning with Errors, this work constructs copy-protection for puncturable cryptographic functionalities and subclasses of evasive functionalities under a stronger security definition, using a new construction of unclonable puncturable obfuscation.

Signatures From Pseudorandom States via ⊥-PRFs Mohammed Barhoush, Amit Behera, Lior Ozer, Louis Salvail, and Or Sattath ASIACRYPT 2025
Abstract and links

This work introduces ⊥-PRGs and ⊥-PRFs whose evaluations are pseudo-deterministic except for a recognizable abort. The main application is a quantum digital-signature scheme with classical public keys and signatures from logarithmic pseudorandom states; the paper also gives CPA-secure public-key encryption with tamper-resilient quantum public keys.

A New World in the Depths of Microcrypt: Separating OWSGs and Quantum Money from QEFID Amit Behera, Giulio Malavolta, Tomoyuki Morimae, Tamer Mour, and Takashi Yamakawa EUROCRYPT 2025
Abstract and links

This paper gives a quantum-unitary oracle relative to which QEFID pairs exist but one-way state generators do not, separating QEFID and one-way puzzles from several efficiently verifiable Microcrypt primitives. It also proves a fully black-box separation of private-key quantum money from QEFID, supporting the view that efficient verification can make a quantum primitive qualitatively stronger.

A Modular Approach to Unclonable Cryptography Prabhanjan Ananth and Amit Behera CRYPTO 2024
Abstract and links

This paper introduces unclonable puncturable obfuscation (UPO) and uses it as a common abstraction for public-key quantum money, copy-protection, unclonable encryption, and single-decryption encryption. It obtains generic copy-protection for puncturably secure functionalities and broad classes of evasive functions, and proposes candidate UPO constructions from cryptographic assumptions.

Pseudorandomness with Proof of Destruction and Applications Amit Behera, Zvika Brakerski, Or Sattath, and Omri Shmueli TCC 2023
Abstract and links

This work introduces pseudorandom states with proofs of destruction: measuring the state produces a secret-key-verifiable classical certificate that the state was destroyed. These states follow from any post-quantum one-way function and let several earlier applications—including encryption, MACs, one-time signatures, commitments, and private quantum coins—use only classical communication.

Noise-Tolerant Quantum Tokens for MAC Amit Behera, Or Sattath, and Uriel Shinar TQC 2022
Abstract and links

A tokenized MAC delegates limited signing authority through single-use quantum tokens, each capable of signing at most one document. This work gives an elementary BB84-based construction tolerating up to 14% noise—the first noise-tolerant TMAC—and proves existential unforgeability against adversaries with signing and verification oracles.

2020
Almost Public Quantum Coins Amit Behera and Or Sattath QIP 2021
Abstract and links

Quantum coins use identical money states rather than serial-numbered bills, offering users greater privacy. This work lifts any private quantum coin scheme into an almost-public scheme in which a new coin is verified against coins already held using projection onto the symmetric subspace, giving the first construction close to public quantum money with provable security from standard assumptions.
